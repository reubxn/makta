# Matka: 2-minute talk track

About 320 words: two minutes at a steady pace (about 160 words a minute). Slide numbers in brackets. Every number below is on the slides and in the app.

**[1] 0:00**
Same cancer, same treatment. Different distance. Matka shows what radiotherapy costs patients in travel, municipality by municipality, and what shorter schedules would remove.

**[2] 0:10**
637,661 people in 89 municipalities live more than 100 km by road from their unit. Lapland has none: from Utsjoki it is 676 km to Oulu, 9 h 22 min each way. The national roadmap itself says palliative radiotherapy is used less beyond 100 km.

**[3] 0:25**
Distance bites at radiotherapy, where the schedule sets the travel: 15 breast sessions from Utsjoki means 16 nights away.

**[4] 0:35**
The evidence: in FAST-Forward, 4,096 patients, 5 sessions in one week is non-inferior to 15 over three. For painful bone metastases, a single 8 Gy session relieves pain as well, with more retreatment, which we count. Yet just 1 of 12 units states its usual breast schedule publicly.

**[5] 0:50**
Matka maps it with a lever; every number clicks through to its source.

**[6–7] 1:00**
At default assumptions, moving about 1,750 eligible breast patients a year from 15 sessions to 5 frees about 17,500 machine sessions and 1,950 nights away. Oulu University Hospital's catchment carries 40% of those nights, the largest share at any commuting threshold from 50 km up.

**[8–9] 1:15**
Three changes. 5 sessions as the default for eligible breast patients, with distance named as a reason. A single 8 Gy session for uncomplicated painful bone metastases. And every unit publishes its schedule mix by distance band, every year. Each has an owner, a 30-day first step and a Goal 3 indicator. We propose a 12-month pilot at OYS that measures side effects and retreatment, not only travel.

**[10–11] 1:35**
AI read unit pages and wrote 22 county memos; it never computes, and 264 of 264 memo numbers trace to the facts. Today's schedule mix is the biggest unknown, so we show a range.

**[12] 1:50**
Next step: one introduction to OYS and Lapland, as non-promotional scientific exchange, and a year of their aggregate data. We can't move the hospital. The schedule can.

## If a judge asks

- **"Is 5 sessions better?"** No: non-inferior, with a similar level of cancer control at 10 years. It applies to whole breast or chest wall without nodal radiotherapy; 15 sessions stays standard when nodes are treated.
- **"Bone: doesn't a single session mean more trips?"** Beyond 100 km, nights away fall from 6.2 to 2.4, but trips stay about the same (2.2 to 2.4) because 20% need retreatment. We show that rather than hide it.
- **"What if units already use 5 sessions?"** Then the gain is smaller: at 50% already on 5 sessions it is about 10,900 sessions a year. Nobody publishes the mix, which is why change 3 exists.
- **"Why OYS?"** It serves Lapland, which has no unit, has the longest drive in Matka's routing (Utsjoki, 676 km), and carries the largest share of nights removed at every commuting threshold from 50 to 300 km (15% at 50 km, 69% at 150 km).
- **"Is the € figure what Kela pays?"** No: it is km at Kela's own-car rate plus its overnight allowance, a lower bound on travel cost. Kela pays less, because patients pay up to 25 € a trip.
- **"What do you want from AstraZeneca?"** One introduction, as non-promotional scientific exchange. OYS and the county own the decision, the pilot and its data.
